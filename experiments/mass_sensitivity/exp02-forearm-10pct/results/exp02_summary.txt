POSITION RANGE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Position ranges remained broadly similar between the baseline and modified
recordings. The largest range change was observed for Shoulder Lift at
approximately +0.14%.

INTERPRETATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The +10% forearm mass modification produced relatively small changes in
recorded joint position ranges but substantially different peak velocity and
especially peak acceleration statistics for several joints.

The strongest recorded response occurred at Wrist 2, where the maximum
absolute velocity increased by 40.66% and the derived maximum absolute
acceleration increased by 517.48%.

These results describe the recorded trajectory statistics. They should not
be interpreted as a direct causal measurement of mechanical torque or
dynamic load without additional synchronized dynamic measurements.

LIMITATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The baseline and experiment recordings have slightly different durations
and sample counts. Therefore the comparison is not a point-by-point
time-synchronized trajectory error analysis.

Acceleration was numerically derived from recorded joint velocity using
numpy.gradient(). Consequently, acceleration peaks can be sensitive to
sampling and measurement noise.

CONCLUSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Experiment 02 successfully completed the intended +10% forearm-mass
perturbation.

The experiment provides a valid dataset for comparison with the 8.3930 kg
baseline and demonstrates that the mass perturbation can be associated with
substantial changes in recorded velocity and derived acceleration statistics,
particularly in the wrist and elbow joints.
